Shopify vs Etsy in the UK: What a £130 Order Really Costs

A UK maker selling a £130 item faces two very different bills: Etsy's percentage on every sale, or Shopify's flat monthly subscription plus a small card fee. The right choice depends on how many orders you sell a month — and the maths is simpler than most sellers expect.

Etsy in the UK takes its fee in four pieces. The transaction fee is 6.5%: £8.45 on a £130 order. Payment processing is 4% plus £0.20: £5.40. Then there is the listing fee of £0.20, plus a regulatory operating fee of 0.48%: £0.62. Add them up and Etsy takes £14.67 on a £130 order, leaving £115.33 before the cost of materials, packaging, and postage. If the sale came through Offsite Ads, add another 15% (£19.50) — or 12% (£15.60) once the shop has made $10,000 in the prior year.

Shopify in the UK works differently: a flat £25 a month for the Basic plan, plus 2% plus 25p per order in card fees — £2.85 on a £130 order. There is no per-order percentage anywhere near Etsy's, so the maths flips with volume. At about 3 orders a month, the £25 subscription is covered by the £11.82 saved on each order versus Etsy, and every order after that is cheaper on your own store. Below that volume, Etsy's per-order cost is still the cheaper way to sell — and Etsy brings the buyers, which a new store does not.

The honest test is per-order, not per-platform.
